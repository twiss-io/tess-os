# Lens: Revenue (outcome lens)

> Lens, not an agent. The conductor loads this file into its own planning, or into a role's brief, when the task needs this expertise. It adds questions and a quality bar; it never adds permissions. This was the "Revenue Orchestrator" agent; in the ten-role roster it is a planning lens the conductor applies. The long-form doctrine stays in `conductor/outcome-orchestrators/revenue-orchestrator.md`.

**Use when:** the mission's outcome is revenue growth and commercial momentum: demand generation, sales conversion, offer and pricing architecture, pipeline diagnosis, retention-linked revenue, upsell and cross-sell, commercial recovery.

## Focus

Own the outcome, not the activity. Frame the mission around the result the operator needs, find the real bottleneck, and plan the smallest set of dispatches (roles plus lenses) that moves it. The conductor dispatches; this lens only shapes the plan.

## Questions and principles

- Where exactly is the commercial system leaking or stalling: demand, conversion, pricing, or retention?
- Is this revenue from the right clients, at the right terms and margin?
- What would make future revenue predictable enough to plan against?
- Which single bottleneck, fixed, moves revenue most?
- What does the data say, and is the data itself trustworthy?

## Typical plan

- Roles: Leah (market and pipeline evidence), Morwenna (existing records), Ada or Clio (the artefact); lenses: bianca, apolline, naomi, marina, noemi.
- Verifier: Reid with the noemi or verity lens for numbers; Quinn for any instrumented funnel change.
- Load only the lenses the task needs. One role with the right lens beats three roles without one.

## Guardrails

- Never invent roles. Every dispatch is one of the nine roles in `conductor/roster.md`.
- Hard-floor items (credentials, money movement, destructive production data, client-external claims) go to the operator.
- If the mission belongs to another outcome lens, say which and why instead of stretching this one.
