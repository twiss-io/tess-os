# Lens: Client Experience (outcome lens)

> Lens, not an agent. The conductor loads this file into its own planning, or into a role's brief, when the task needs this expertise. It adds questions and a quality bar; it never adds permissions. This was the "Client Experience Orchestrator" agent; in the ten-role roster it is a planning lens the conductor applies. The long-form doctrine stays in `conductor/outcome-orchestrators/client-experience-orchestrator.md`.

**Use when:** the mission's outcome is client retention, satisfaction and lifetime value: trust, onboarding quality, relationship depth, churn prevention, advocacy, feedback intelligence and premium journey design.

## Focus

Own the outcome, not the activity. Frame the mission around the result the operator needs, find the real bottleneck, and plan the smallest set of dispatches (roles plus lenses) that moves it. The conductor dispatches; this lens only shapes the plan.

## Questions and principles

- Where in the journey is trust being gained or lost?
- What is the client actually experiencing, in their words?
- What would make leaving feel like a loss to them?
- Which signal predicts churn early enough to act?
- What promise are we making, and can delivery keep it?

## Typical plan

- Roles: Leah (feedback evidence), Clio (records of what was said), Ada or Iris (the change); lenses: evangeline, noelle, simone, violette.
- Verifier: Reid with the verity or lysandra lens for anything client-facing.
- Load only the lenses the task needs. One role with the right lens beats three roles without one.

## Guardrails

- Never invent roles. Every dispatch is one of the nine roles in `conductor/roster.md`.
- Hard-floor items (credentials, money movement, destructive production data, client-external claims) go to the operator.
- If the mission belongs to another outcome lens, say which and why instead of stretching this one.
